Pitch for the Toronto Fintech Pitch Night judges

RenewalCheck

A free tool that tells a Canadian homeowner, in two minutes, what their mortgage payment will be at renewal and what to do about it.

John Khan · October 2026

Audience: early-stage judges and housing-policy reviewers. Built only on public Bank of Canada and CMHC data.

1 / 10
The problem

Priya signed in 2021. Her renewal letter just arrived.

  • She locked a five-year fixed rate when the Bank of Canada's policy rate was 0.25%.
  • Her lender's letter gives a new rate and a 15-day window to sign.
  • She has no quick way to see the new payment, compare it, or know whether to switch, shorten the term or prepay.
  • Most people in her spot sign whatever the letter says.

Illustration (hypothetical household)

$500,000 borrowed in late 2021, 25-year amortization

Payment at 4.79%: $2,849/month

Renews at 6.19% on $441,063: $3,188/month

+$340 a month. Rates are the Bank of Canada's posted 5-year conventional rate, which is higher than most negotiated rates.

Rates: Bank of Canada, 5-year conventional mortgage rate (series V80691335): 4.79% in Nov 2021, 6.19% on Oct 14, 2026. bankofcanada.ca. Payment math: standard Canadian semi-annual compounding.

Why now

The rate floor of 2020 to 2022 is gone, and those mortgages are coming due

Even after cuts, today's policy rate is nine times the 2021 level. Renewals in 2025 to 2027 reset from the lowest rates on record.

Source: Bank of Canada, target for the overnight rate (series V39079), month-end values, Valet API, retrieved Oct 15, 2026. bankofcanada.ca/core-functions/monetary-policy/key-interest-rate

3 / 10
The solution

Enter four numbers. See your renewal, plainly.

  1. Balance, rate, payment and renewal date from your statement.
  2. RenewalCheck pulls current posted rates and the policy rate straight from the Bank of Canada.
  3. It shows the new payment, the yearly difference and three options: keep your payment and extend, shorten the term, or prepay.
  4. It prints a one-page checklist to take to your lender or broker.

What it never does

  • No sign-up, no email, no data stored
  • No product sales or referral fees
  • No figure without a linked public source

All rate inputs come from the Bank of Canada Valet API (public, free): bankofcanada.ca/valet/docs

Sustainability

Free for homeowners, funded like a public good

  • Grants from financial-literacy and housing programs
  • Sponsorships from credit unions and non-profits, shown clearly, with no say over results
  • Licensing a white-label version to community legal clinics and credit counsellors

Costs are small by design. Static site, no database, public data feeds. Hosting runs on free tiers today.

The constraint is trust, not servers, so the rule stays: no lead selling, ever.

Hosting: GitHub Pages (free). Data: Bank of Canada Valet API and CMHC publications (free, public).
